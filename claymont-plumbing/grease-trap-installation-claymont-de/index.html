    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Claymont

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more once excavation and site restoration are counted. Brand is a small part of it. The written estimate holds unless the dig turns up a buried surprise.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. Factory terms vary, so we hand you the registration card or file it for you. Keep your cleaning logs. Some manufacturers ask for proof of regular service before they honor a claim on a cracked body or a failed baffle.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We add up the drain rate of each sink compartment, floor sink and dish machine, then apply the code's drain-down time to get gallons per minute. Gravity tanks use volume instead. For those, the local pretreatment program often has its own formula based on seats or fixture units, and we use theirs.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    Coverage comes from two places. The manufacturer warrants the trap body and lid, and the term depends on the brand and whether it is steel or plastic. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap. Leave room to open the lid and clean it without moving equipment. Never under a cook line. Outdoor interceptors go where a pump truck can park within reach of the hose and where the lids will not end up under a dumpster or a parked car.
