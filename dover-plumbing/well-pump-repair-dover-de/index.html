    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Dover

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. A properly sized tank can add years to pump life.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Dover

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. No water, low pressure or a pump that hums without moving water are the usual signs one has failed. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Submersible Pump Pulling and Repair in Dover

    Sand wears impellers down over time, and a pump that once filled the tank in a minute starts taking three. The motor, the wire splice and the check valve are the other common failures. Before pulling anything, we test the motor and wire from the wellhead. New pumps match your well's yield.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Dover, DE

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets Delaware code when the job is done.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Dover, DE

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. After flooding, wells should be tested and septic systems checked before normal use, since floodwater can contaminate both. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. Arrestors are cheap.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Dover, DE

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. GNV Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.
