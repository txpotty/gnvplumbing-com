    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Dover, DE

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is thinner and mostly used inside the house. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against stone contact and soil chemistry. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Commercial Water Line Services in Dover, DE

    Commercial services run bigger, often 1-1/2 to 2 inches, and almost always have a backflow assembly that needs testing after any work. We schedule the shutdown around your hours.     Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Upgrades in Dover, DE

    Street pressure varies a lot from one neighborhood to the next. Code says no more than 80 psi inside the house. If your new line comes in above that, we add a pressure reducing valve right after the main shutoff, set it around 55 to 65 psi, and swap the old gate valve for a full-port ball valve. A quarter turn and the water is off.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Dover

    We buy PEX in long coils. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, since a single coil reaches all the way without a splice. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    Copper Water Line Installation in Dover

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. Street pressure does not bother it. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. PEX is cheaper.

    Water Line Upgrades and Code Compliance in Dover, DE

    A lot of older houses were fed with a 1/2-inch or 3/4-inch line that was fine for one bathroom. It cannot keep up with two baths and a washer running. When we replace the line we size it to your fixture count, which often means going to 1-inch, and bring the shutoff, depth and backflow pieces up to what code asks for today. Better pressure upstairs.

    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Dover, DE

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside when exposed to chlorinated water. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some home insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Worth replacing.
