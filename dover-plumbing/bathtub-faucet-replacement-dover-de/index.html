    Drips at the spout come from inside the valve. Single-handle drips mean worn cartridge seals, and multi-handle drips mean worn washers and seats. New internals end the drip. Cracked bodies get a new valve.

    Hard Water Tub Faucet Replacement in Dover

    Tub Spout Replacement in Dover

    Spouts mount two ways. Slip-on spouts clamp to copper with a set screw, and threaded ones screw onto a pipe nipple. The connection gets checked first. Diverter spouts must match too.

    Thermostatic and Pressure-Balance Valves in Dover, DE

    Pressure-balance valves react to pressure drops. When someone flushes a toilet, the valve cuts hot and cold flow in proportion so the mix stays steady, and a thermostatic valve goes further by holding a set temperature even as the water heater cools. Either one satisfies scald rules. Thermostatic costs more.

    Delta Tub Faucet Replacement in Dover, DE

    Delta tub valves are some of the easiest to update. Many Delta Monitor valves accept a new cartridge and trim from the front, and the MultiChoice universal rough-in lets you switch trim styles later without touching the pipes in the wall. Popular Delta cartridges are on the truck. Older Delta valves sometimes need full replacement.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Bathtub Faucet Replacement in Dover

    Plan on $200 to $600 for most jobs. Faucet type and pipe work set the price, and premium trim or thermostatic valves cost more. That range covers labor and a basic faucet.

    A worn cartridge or stem washer is the usual cause. Single-handle drips mean cartridge seals, and multi-handle drips mean washers and seats. New parts stop it.

    A slip-on spout is a fair DIY job. Anything inside the wall is different, because replacing a valve means soldering or crimping in a tight cavity full of wood and then pressure-testing it before the wall closes, and a mistake there leaks out of sight. Leave valves to a plumber.

    Plan on three to five hours. The wall has to be opened, the old valve cut out, the new valve soldered or crimped in and pressure-tested, and then the trim and any access panel or remodel plate installed. Tile repair adds time.

    We try to. Most faucet makers sell trim in chrome, brushed nickel, matte black and oil-rubbed bronze, and we can usually match the tub trim to your sink faucet and towel bars from the same brand line. Brushed finishes vary slightly.

    A drip that comes back after a new cartridge is the big one. Watch for stiff or spinning handles, temperature swings, and crust that keeps coming back. Any two point toward a new valve.

    Private wells are common in rural Delaware, and they can carry iron, nitrates, or low pH that calls for testing and treatment. Calcium and magnesium build up on cartridge seals and inside the spout diverter. That buildup makes handles stiff, wears the rubber seals faster than normal, and eventually causes drips or a diverter that will not lift, which shortens the life of the whole valve. Regular cleaning slows it down.

    Do not crank the handle shut. Cartridge valves seal with light pressure, and forcing the handle past the stop wears the seals and the stop itself, so a gentle turn and an occasional wipe of the trim with a soft cloth go a long way. Skip harsh cleaners on the finish.
