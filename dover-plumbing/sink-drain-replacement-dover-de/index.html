    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. The old body comes out, the seat gets cleaned, and the new flange goes in sealed with a clevis set for easy lifting. Finish matches the faucet. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    P-Trap Replacement in Dover

    Thin chrome traps are the ones we replace most. They rust inside and their nuts seize, so we cut them out and fit heavy-wall PVC or polished brass. Then it gets a full flow test. Total time is often under an hour.

    Emergency Sink Drain Service in Dover

    A trap that falls apart sends a sink of water into the cabinet. Turn off the faucet, set a bucket under it, and stop using that sink, and if the drain is backed up into a second fixture, stop using water elsewhere in the house too. We send a plumber with trap kits. Most get fixed for good on the first visit.

    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Dover

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Bowls without an overflow need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, or water leaks out the slots. Mismatched drains are common. Swapping it takes under an hour.

    Bathroom Sink Drain Replacement in Dover, DE

    Bathroom drains wear out at the pop-up. Pivot nuts leak and drain bodies corrode at the overflow holes, so a sink that will not hold water needs a new pop-up. Patching one piece rarely works. The new drain matches your faucet finish.

    Trap Arm and Wall Drain Repair in Dover, DE

    Leaks right at the wall point to the trap arm or stub. A cracked arm or rusted stub drips down the back panel, out of sight, until the wood swells or smells. The bad section gets cut back. Then new PVC gets tied in.

    Leaking P-Trap Replacement in Dover

    Traps leak at the joints first. The beveled washers inside the slip nuts flatten and harden over the years, and a trap that has been taken apart several times to clear clogs often has stripped plastic threads or a cracked nut. A new trap kit costs little. Labor is most of the bill.

    Push-Button Drain Installation in Dover, DE

    Push-button drains get rid of the lift rod. A press on the cap opens and closes them, and a slotted body only goes in sinks with an overflow. Cleaning is simpler with no linkage. Hair still collects, so pull the cap now and then.
