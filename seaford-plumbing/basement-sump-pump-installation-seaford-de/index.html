    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Seaford

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Pour a five gallon bucket of water into the pit, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. That is the whole test.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. A new pit with slab cutting can stretch it to a full day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. We tell you up front.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Most local codes do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can draw a fine.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. The pump carries its own manufacturer warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Save the paperwork.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. A tune-up covers the float, the pit, the check valve and the discharge, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. The visit is short.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. Each offers cast iron housings and dependable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. The pit size picks the model.
