    Spot Repair Through the Slab in Georgetown, DE

    Tunneling Slab Leak Repair in Georgetown, DE

    In some cases, especially when the leak is under a load-bearing wall or in a hard-to-reach area, tunneling beneath the foundation is the preferred method.     Slab Leak Reroute in Georgetown

    Rerouting is often the best long-term choice, especially with older copper lines. We abandon the leaking line under the slab and run a new PEX line through the attic and walls. Openings are small and placed where patching is simplest. Reroutes end future leaks in that section of pipe.

    Direct Slab Penetration Repair in Georgetown, DE

    When a slab leak is directly accessible and localized, direct slab penetration is often the most practical repair method. This involves carefully cutting a small section of your concrete slab to access and repair or replace the damaged pipe section. Our team makes sure the repair is fully compliant with Delaware plumbing codes and the slab is correctly restored, preventing future issues. We take great care to contain dust and debris during this process in your Georgetown home.

    Hot Water Slab Leak Repair in Georgetown, DE

    Hot water lines leak under slabs more often than cold lines because heat expands and contracts the pipe against concrete and gravel. Mineral scale and constant recirculation can wear the pipe wall faster. We repair the leak or reroute the hot line, and we can adjust recirculation pumps to reduce wear. Warm floors and higher gas or electric bills are common signs.

    Cold Water Slab Leak Repair in Georgetown

    Cold water slab leaks are harder to feel but still waste water and wet the soil. They often show up as a high water bill or a meter that moves with all fixtures off. We isolate the cold line, find the leak and repair or reroute it. Fixing cold leaks quickly helps protect your foundation.

    Slab Leak Pipe Rerouting in Georgetown

    For more complex or recurring slab leaks, or when the original piping is badly deteriorated, pipe rerouting is an effective long-term solution. This method involves abandoning the leaky pipe under the slab and installing new plumbing lines through walls or the attic, bypassing the foundation entirely. This removes future slab leak risks from that particular line and is often preferred for older homes in Georgetown with aging copper or galvanized pipes.
