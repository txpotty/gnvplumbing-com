    Water Heater Replacement in Georgetown

    Most tank heaters give out between 8 and 12 years, sooner with heavy sediment. We also check whether you need an expansion tank. One visit covers most installs.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Georgetown, DE

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. The fix is a sealed basin with an ejector pump that lifts waste to the main. Pump and basin are sized to the fixtures, the basin is vented to code, and the discharge gets a check valve and a shutoff so the pump can come out for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    Whole-House Water Softener Installation in Georgetown

    Water in Delaware ranges from moderately soft to hard depending on the source, and groundwater areas often see more scale on fixtures. Softener sizing comes down to hardness times daily gallons, which tells us how many grains the resin has to remove between regenerations. A unit sized too small regenerates constantly and wastes salt. The drain line gets a proper air gap. Then we program the regeneration schedule to your water and run a test cycle.

    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Georgetown, DE

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. An expansion tank absorbs that swing and keeps the relief valve from dripping. A PRV usually means you need one. Fixtures below the sewer line get an ejector system with a sealed basin, a check valve and a vent.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown, DE

    Electric tankless heaters pull serious amperage, often 120 amps or more for a whole-house unit. Before we quote one, we check that your panel and service can carry that load, and we bring in a licensed electrician when they cannot. Plumbing is the easy part. We set the flow rate and outlet temperature so the shower does not run cold when the dishwasher kicks on.

    Sump Pump Installation in Georgetown, DE

    A sump pump that fails during a storm usually failed months earlier: a stuck float, a seized impeller, a dead check valve or a cord unplugged for the shop vac. The pit goes to proper depth, the primary pump is sized for the inflow, and a battery backup is added on request. Where the water exits matters most. We run the discharge line far enough from the foundation that the water does not cycle straight back into the pit.

    Backflow Assembly Testing and Installation in Georgetown

    Fire lines and irrigation systems account for most of the backflow assemblies we install. Each one is tested right after installation by a certified backflow tester, and the report goes to the water purveyor. That starts the annual clock. Retesting is usually yearly, and we keep a reminder list so you do not miss the date.

    PEX Pipe Installation and Repiping in Georgetown

    Pinhole leaks in copper keep coming back once the pipe walls start to thin. Repiping in PEX stops the cycle. We run either a manifold with home runs to each fixture, which balances pressure, or a trunk-and-branch layout when the walls make home runs impractical. Access holes are kept small and patched, or left square and clean for your drywall contractor.
