    Frequently Asked Questions About Garbage Disposal Replacement in Georgetown

    The cost for garbage disposal replacement in Georgetown, DE typically ranges from $200 to $600. This includes the new unit itself, which can vary from $100 for a basic 1/3 HP model to over $300 for a high-end 1 HP unit, plus labor costs. Factors like the disposal's horsepower, brand, and any necessary plumbing modifications can affect the final price.

    Most garbage disposal replacements can be completed within 1 to 2 hours. This timeframe includes disconnecting the old unit, installing the new one, and testing it thoroughly. More complex situations, such as significant plumbing modifications or electrical work, might extend the duration slightly.

    If your current disposal jams often or struggles with food waste, a 3/4 or 1 HP model can help.

    For light use, a 1/3 HP or 1/2 HP unit is usually sufficient. For regular use and tough food scraps, a 3/4 HP or 1 HP unit is recommended. Our plumbers can help you assess your needs and choose the best fit for your Georgetown home.

    A continuous feed disposal operates when you flip a wall switch, allowing you to add food waste while it's running. A batch feed disposal only operates when a special stopper is inserted into the drain, making it generally safer as it prevents accidental activation while hands are near the opening.

    While it's possible for a handy homeowner to replace a garbage disposal, it involves working with both electricity and plumbing, which can be hazardous if not done correctly. Improper installation can lead to leaks, electrical shorts, and voided warranties. For safety and peace of mind, hiring a professional plumber is recommended.

    If your disposal is old, frequently leaking, or making grinding noises without grinding food, replacement is usually more cost-effective and provides a longer-lasting solution. Minor issues like a simple jam can often be repaired.

    Key signs you need a new garbage disposal include persistent leaks from the unit's housing, a humming noise without grinding, frequent clogs despite proper usage, foul odors that won't go away, or if the unit simply won't turn on. These symptoms usually indicate internal motor or component failure.
