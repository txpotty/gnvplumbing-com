    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown, DE

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and replacement parts are widely stocked.     Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown, DE

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Georgetown, DE homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. Panel capacity gets checked up front.

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown

    Navien builds condensing tankless units with stainless heat exchangers, and several models come with a recirculation pump built in. That pump can push hot water toward a far bathroom on a schedule, which shortens the wait at the tap without adding a separate pump under the sink. Navien vents in PVC. Condensing units also need a condensate drain.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown

    Propane homes in Georgetown are good candidates for tankless, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. Tank size matters too.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Georgetown

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job because the old tank spot is not always the best place. A garage or outside wall near the gas meter can shorten the gas run and simplify venting, but it may lengthen the hot water run to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Georgetown

    Gas tankless units in Georgetown are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. The catch is gas supply. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We test the new gas piping before first fire.
