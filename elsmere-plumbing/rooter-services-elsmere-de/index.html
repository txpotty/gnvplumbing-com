    Slow drains, gurgling pipes and sewer smell are the early ones. Water backing up in a tub when you flush the toilet means the main line is involved. Do not wait on that one. Several fixtures acting up at once almost always points to a main line clog rather than a single trap under one sink.

    Rooting uses a spinning cable with cutting blades to break up clogs and roots. Jetting works another way. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scrub the pipe walls clean. Many tough lines do best with both, cut first to open the line and jetted second to wash out what the blades left.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. Cables can kink, whip and wrap around a wrist in a second. They also get stuck. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.

    Gurgling toilets, sewage odors and slow drains in several fixtures are the common signs. Water backing up into a tub when you flush is another clue. That one is serious. Repeat clogs in one line usually mean a deeper problem, often roots at a joint well out in the yard.

    No permit is needed for routine rooting. Installing a new cleanout, digging up a broken section or replacing the sewer lateral usually does require a plumbing permit and an inspection. Permits for repair work are on us.
