    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Millsboro

    Costs range from about $200 for simple thawing to $1,500 or more for a burst pipe inside a finished wall. Where the pipe sits matters most. A frozen line in an open crawlspace is quick work, while a split pipe in a second-floor ceiling means opening drywall, repairing the pipe and leaving the opening ready for a drywall patch.

    No pipe is freeze-proof. PEX tolerates freezing better than most. It can stretch slightly as ice forms instead of splitting the way copper does, but the fittings at each end can still crack, so location and insulation matter more than the pipe itself.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Seal and insulate around exposed lines. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    No water at one faucet after a cold night is the classic sign. Frost on the pipe confirms it. An unusually cold or bulging section is usually where the ice is.

    For an exposed pipe you can reach, yes, carefully. Open the faucet first. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or a space heater kept a safe distance away, working from the faucet back toward the frozen section, and never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame near framing.

    Hard freezes are usually short here, but a single cold night can still split an outdoor faucet left connected to a hose. Any pipe in an unheated space can freeze after several hours below 32 degrees. Houses built for mild winters are often at higher risk, because their pipes may run through attics and outside walls with little insulation. Cold snaps catch people off guard.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We will look at why it froze again and quote insulation, heat cable or a reroute into a heated space, because patching the same exposed run every winter costs more than fixing the cause once.

    Repairs carry a written workmanship warranty, with the term on your invoice. Manufacturer warranties cover parts. The warranty covers our repair, not a new freeze, so if the same line freezes again because it is still exposed, we will talk with you about insulation, heat cable or rerouting so it does not keep happening.
