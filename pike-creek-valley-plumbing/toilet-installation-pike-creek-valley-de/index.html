    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Pike Creek Valley

    Most homeowners in Pike Creek Valley, DE pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. Removing a stubborn old unit adds time. Rusted bolts get cut off, and a cast iron flange that cracks on the way out needs a repair ring, both adding labor.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the toilet drain. Twelve inches is the most common. You will also see 10 and 14 inch rough-ins, and buying the wrong one means the toilet will not fit or sits well off the wall. Measure from the wall itself, not the baseboard.

    A one-piece toilet is cast as a single unit, so there is no tank-to-bowl joint to leak. Expect a higher price and more weight. Two-piece toilets join tank and bowl with a gasket, so they are easier to carry upstairs and you can replace one half if it cracks. Both flush well.

    For most adults, yes. Sitting and standing take less effort. It helps seniors and anyone after knee or hip surgery, but young children and short adults may like a standard bowl better.

    Two warranties apply. The manufacturer covers the toilet. Most brands give one to five years on parts like the fill valve and flapper and a longer term, sometimes lifetime, on the vitreous china, while our own guarantee covers any leak traced back to how we set it.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Some cracks are hard to see at first. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    We guarantee the workmanship on every toilet we set. A leak from our work gets fixed free. That covers the seal and the supply connection, while manufacturing defects in the toilet fall under the maker's warranty, which we help you file if a part fails early. Hang on to your receipt.
