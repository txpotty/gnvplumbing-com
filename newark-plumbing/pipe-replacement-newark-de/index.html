    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Choices in Newark, DE

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Newark, DE

    Galvanized steel was common before the 1960s. Inside, rust builds once the coating fails. Over decades the opening shrinks, pressure drops and the water turns brown after sitting, and the threads where pipes join are often the first to leak. Replacing it is the only fix.

    Whole-Home Re-Piping in Newark

    A whole-home repipe replaces every supply line from the main shutoff to each fixture, including the water heater connections. Most single-story homes take two to three days. We run new trunk lines through the attic or crawlspace, branch down to fixtures through small wall openings, with service restored each evening when possible. Then patching.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Newark, DE

    Removing every piece is rarely needed. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, so we avoid tearing open walls just to pull out dead pipe. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Newark, DE

    Copper still has fans. It is durable and heat tolerant. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, and support every run with hangers at the spacing code requires, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

    Commercial Pipe Replacement in Newark

    Commercial buildings often have larger supply lines and more fixtures. Work has to fit around your hours. We plan pipe replacement in phases whenever the layout allows it, so only part of the building is offline at a time. Night work available.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in Newark, DE

    Pinhole leaks start small. First a damp spot, then drywall damage. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, and when one shows up, more are often on the way along the same lines. Repipe or patch, we explain.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Newark

    Apartments and condos share risers. One leak can affect several units. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, notify tenants ahead of shutoffs, and test each section before moving on. Organized work.

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Newark

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.
