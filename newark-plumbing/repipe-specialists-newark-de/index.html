    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Newark, DE

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. Brittle pipe walls and cracked fittings mean one polybutylene leak usually signals risk across the whole house. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Newark, DE

    Private wells are common in rural Delaware, and they can carry iron, nitrates, or low pH that calls for testing and treatment. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. For homes with hard water, we usually recommend PEX-A, which resists scale and does not corrode, and we can add a softener loop or a whole-house filter during the repipe. Adding the loop now costs less than later.

    Copper Repiping in Newark, DE

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. We install Type L copper, which has a thicker wall than the Type M used in many older homes, with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings. Copper takes a bit longer. Copper repipes usually cost more and take a day longer than PEX, mostly because every turn needs a fitting.

    Commercial Repiping in Newark, DE

    Commercial buildings in Newark, DE often have larger pipe, risers that serve several floors and tenants who cannot lose water for a full day. We phase commercial repipes by floor, suite or riser so most of the building stays in service while we work. Night work is possible. Materials are usually Type L copper or commercial PEX, sized to the fixture count and installed to commercial code.

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through.
