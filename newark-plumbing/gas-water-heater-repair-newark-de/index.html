    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Newark

    Most gas water heaters built in recent decades have a flammable vapor ignition resistant design. A sensor or flame arrestor screen shuts the burner down if it senses vapors or overheating. Lint and dust in garages can clog the arrestor and trip the system. We clean the combustion chamber and replace sensors or thermal switches as required.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Newark, DE

    Issues with the gas valve or thermostat can lead to inconsistent water temperatures or a complete lack of hot water. Our team can test these crucial components for proper operation, checking for electrical continuity and gas flow. If a part is defective, we replace it with a quality, manufacturer-approved component so your water heater heats efficiently and safely.

    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Newark

    A common problem with gas water heaters is a pilot light that won't stay lit or a burner that fails to ignite. This can be caused by a faulty thermocouple, a dirty pilot tube or issues with the gas control valve. Our technicians in Newark are adept at diagnosing these components and performing the necessary repairs, whether it is cleaning, adjusting or replacing parts. We ensure proper gas flow and ignition for consistent hot water.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Newark, DE

    The gas control valve controls temperature and shuts off gas if the pilot goes out. Newer valves from Honeywell and White-Rodgers use status lights that blink codes when something fails. If the valve itself has failed, we replace it with an exact match or listed universal model. We leak test and confirm temperature control afterward.

    Tank Leaks & Pressure Problems in Newark

    Even gas water heaters can develop tank leaks, often due to corrosion from hard water or high water pressure. Private wells are common in rural Delaware, and they can carry iron, nitrates, or acidic pH that calls for testing and treatment. While some leaks may call for a replacement, minor leaks or pressure relief valve issues can often be repaired. Our team assesses the extent of the damage, inspects the anode rod and addresses pressure imbalances to prevent further problems in your Newark home.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Newark

    Power vent heaters use a blower to push exhaust out through PVC pipe. Failures usually involve the blower motor, pressure switch, hot surface igniter or condensate in the vent. We read the error codes, test each part and repair the failure. Correct vent slope and termination are checked before we leave.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Troubleshooting in Newark

    Odd noises coming from your gas water heater, such as rumbling, popping or whistling, often indicate sediment buildup in the tank or a burner problem. Hard water can speed up sediment accumulation. Our repair service includes flushing the tank and inspecting for sediment, addressing pressure issues or checking other components that might be causing the noise, improving efficiency and quiet operation.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Water Heater Repair in Newark

    The cost of gas water heater repair in Newark, DE can vary widely depending on the issue. Minor repairs like a thermocouple replacement might range from $150 to $350, while more complex issues such as a gas valve replacement could be $400 to $800 or more. We always provide a detailed, upfront estimate after diagnosis.

    Most repairs are finished the same day in one to two hours. Special order gas valves may take one to three business days.

    Regular maintenance, such as annual flushing of the tank to remove sediment, inspecting the anode rod and checking the pressure relief valve, can significantly extend the life of your gas water heater and prevent common issues. Ask about a maintenance plan with GNV Plumbing.

    Gas tank water heaters typically last 8 to 12 years. Hard water sediment and skipped maintenance can shorten that.

    Most common gas water heater repairs, such as replacing a thermocouple or thermostat, can be finished within 1 to 3 hours. More complex issues or those requiring special parts might take longer, but we always aim for same-day resolution whenever possible.

    Many Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves use flash codes to report problems like a weak thermopile or a tripped sensor. We read the code and test the related part.

    Look out for a lack of hot water, inconsistent water temperature, a pilot light that won't stay lit, unusual noises like rumbling or popping, a rotten egg smell, which can point to a gas leak, or visible leaks around the tank. Any of these warrant a professional inspection.

    A thermocouple is a safety device in a gas water heater that senses if the pilot light is lit. If it doesn't detect heat, it shuts off the gas supply to prevent gas leaks. A faulty thermocouple is a leading reason for a pilot light not staying lit and is a critical safety component.
