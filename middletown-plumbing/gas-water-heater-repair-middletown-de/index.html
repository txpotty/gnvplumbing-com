    Regular maintenance, such as annual flushing of the tank to remove sediment, inspecting the anode rod and checking the pressure relief valve, can significantly extend the life of your gas water heater and prevent common issues. Ask about a maintenance plan with GNV Plumbing.

    Because of the dangers that come with natural gas and possible carbon monoxide poisoning, gas water heater repairs should always be handled by a licensed professional. DIY attempts can be dangerous and may void warranties or break local Middletown, DE plumbing codes.

    In most cases, minor repairs like replacing a thermocouple or a thermostat do not require a permit.     Gas tank water heaters usually last 8 to 12 years. Hard water sediment and skipped maintenance can cut that.

    A thermocouple is a safety device in a gas water heater that senses if the pilot light is lit. If it doesn't sense heat, it shuts off the gas supply to prevent gas leaks. A faulty thermocouple is a leading reason for a pilot light not staying lit and is a critical safety component.

    On average, a well-maintained gas water heater can last 10 to 12 years. Regular maintenance, including flushing the tank, can help extend its lifespan. If your unit is nearing this age and having frequent issues, replacement might be more cost-effective than continuous repairs.
