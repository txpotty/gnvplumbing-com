    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in New Castle, DE

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside when exposed to chlorinated water. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some insurers will not write a policy on a house that still has it. Worth replacing.

    PEX Water Line Replacement in New Castle

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster, which matters on a long run. Scale and corrosion are not a problem with it. We use PEX-a or PEX rated for direct burial, connect it with brass or poly fittings that are approved underground, and lay tracer wire on top so a locator can find it later, since plastic pipe does not show up on a metal detector. Cheap insurance.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in New Castle

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    PEX Water Line Installation in New Castle, DE

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Freezing weather arrives most winters in Delaware, and pipes in crawlspaces, exterior walls, and unheated garages are the first to freeze. PEX can swell a little in a freeze and go back to shape, where copper splits. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and we keep it out of direct sun at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Copper Water Line Installation in New Castle

    Type K copper is the thick-wall grade made for burial. It handles high street pressure without complaint. We bed it in sand, keep it away from sharp rock, and sleeve it where it passes through a foundation wall, because concrete and copper rubbing together for twenty years eventually wear a hole. PEX is cheaper.

    Water Line Upgrades and Code Compliance in New Castle, DE

    A lot of older houses were fed with a 1/2-inch or 3/4-inch line that was fine for one bathroom. Add a second bath and a dishwasher and it falls short. When we replace the line we size it to your fixture count, which often means going to 1-inch, and bring the shutoff, depth and backflow pieces up to what code asks for today. Better pressure upstairs.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in New Castle

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so the full line costs less than a string of repairs. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.
