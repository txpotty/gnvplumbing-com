    Toilet Flush Mechanism Repair in New Castle, DE

    If your toilet isn't flushing properly, it could be due to issues with the flush chain, handle, flapper, or fill valve. A weak flush might mean the tank isn't filling correctly, while a loose handle might not be engaging the flapper. Our plumbers are adept at diagnosing these internal tank problems, replacing worn or broken components like flush valves, fill valves, and toilet handles to restore full and effective flushing power.

    Leaking Toilet Repair in New Castle

    Toilet leaks can cause serious water damage to your bathroom floor, subfloor, and even the ceiling below. Leaks can start from the base of the toilet, the tank, or the supply line. Common culprits include a worn-out wax ring, loose bolts, or cracked porcelain. We thoroughly inspect all potential leak points, replacing seals, tightening connections, or recommending bowl replacement if the porcelain is damaged, preventing further water intrusion.

    Cracked Tank and Supply Line Repair in New Castle, DE

    A hairline crack in the tank or a bulging braided supply line can let go without warning and flood a bathroom. We inspect the tank, tank-to-bowl bolts and gasket, and the supply line on every visit. If the porcelain is cracked we will price a replacement tank or new toilet, and we upgrade old plastic or vinyl supply lines to braided stainless.

    Running Toilet Repair in New Castle

    A running toilet is not just annoying, it can waste hundreds of gallons of water a day, leading to significantly higher water bills. This common issue is often caused by a faulty flapper, a misaligned fill valve, or an improperly adjusted float. Our plumbers promptly identify the exact cause, whether it's mineral buildup from hard water affecting the flapper seal or a worn-out component, and replace the necessary parts to stop the continuous flow and restore efficiency.

    Clogged Toilet Clearing in New Castle, DE

    Running Toilet Fixes in New Castle

    Fill Valve Replacement in New Castle

    A noisy, slow or hissing tank often points to a worn fill valve. Mineral scale clogs the small ports inside, which causes long refill times and phantom refills in the night. The job takes about 20 minutes and makes an older toilet noticeably quieter.
