    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Bear, DE

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half.     Cracked Toilet Replacement in Bear

    Porcelain cracks below the waterline are not safely repairable, and epoxy fixes on a tank under constant water pressure rarely last more than a few months. You should replace it.     What a Standard Swap Includes in Bear

    The swap itself runs about two hours: water off, tank drained, old toilet out, flange scraped and inspected, new toilet set on a fresh seal, and a braided stainless supply line connected. Then we level it. The base gets caulked, with a small gap left at the back so any future leak shows up instead of hiding under the floor.

    High-Efficiency Toilet (HET) Upgrades in Bear, DE

    High-efficiency toilets carry a WaterSense label and a MaP score, which tells you how many grams of waste the toilet clears in one flush. Look for 800 or higher. A high score means fewer double flushes and fewer clogs, and that is where the real water savings come from.

    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in Bear

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. First we find the model number inside the tank. A cracked bowl always means a full replacement, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Bear

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. Some carriers need new bolts. We match the new bowl to the existing carrier whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.
