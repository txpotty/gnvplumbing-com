    Vessel and Grid Drain Replacement in Bear

    Vessel sinks use a different drain. Most glass and stone vessel bowls have no overflow hole, so they need an unslotted grid or umbrella drain, and a slotted drain in one of those bowls leaks out of the slots right into the counter hole.     Leaking P-Trap Replacement in Bear

    Traps leak at the joints first. The beveled washers inside the slip nuts flatten and harden over the years, and a trap that has been taken apart several times to clear clogs often has stripped plastic threads or a cracked nut. Trap kits are cheap. Labor is most of the bill.

    Pop-Up Drain Replacement in Bear

    Lift-rod pop-ups are the most common bath drain. The old body comes out, the seat gets cleaned, and the new flange goes in sealed with a clevis set for easy lifting. We match the faucet finish. Overflow-style drains are standard.

    Choosing Drain Material in Bear, DE

    PVC is the default choice under most sinks. It does not rust, it stands up to drain cleaners, and it costs less than brass, while polished 17-gauge brass makes sense under a pedestal sink or any trap you can see. Some houses use black ABS instead. We match what you have, with a proper transition where they meet.

    Utility Sink Drain Replacement in Bear

    Laundry tubs see lint and paint water. Over time that wears the thin plastic drain and trap on a utility sink until it cracks, and lint from the washer standpipe often builds up in the trap so badly that it never drains at full speed again. A 1-1/2 inch trap with a cleanout plug goes in. A lint trap on the washer hose helps.

    Trap Arm and Wall Drain Repair in Bear, DE

    Leaks right at the wall point to the trap arm or stub. A cracked arm or rusted stub drips down the back panel, out of sight, until the wood swells or smells. We cut back to solid pipe. Then new PVC gets tied in.

    Corroded Drain Replacement in Bear, DE

    Drain cleaners speed up corrosion. Chemicals left sitting in a metal trap eat through it, so many old traps we pull have pinholes at the bottom. Plastic traps resist that damage. We recommend it for most cabinets.
