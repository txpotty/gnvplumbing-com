    Frequently Asked Questions About Hydro Jetting in Wilmington

    The cost of hydro jetting in Wilmington, DE can range from $300 to $800, depending on the severity of the clog, the length and diameter of the pipe, and whether camera inspection is required. More complex jobs, like removing extensive tree roots or cleaning long commercial lines, may be on the higher end. We provide a written estimate after assessing your situation.

    Hydro jetting is exceptionally effective against almost all types of clogs. It can break through solid obstructions like tree roots, flush out heavy grease and food buildup, clear mineral scale, and remove compacted sludge and debris that traditional snaking often misses.

    GNV Plumbing uses professional-grade hydro jetting equipment from established manufacturers like Spartan Tool and MyTana. These brands are known for their reliability, powerful performance, and safety features, so we can deliver the most effective cleaning for your pipes.

    It is safe for most pipes in fair condition when pressure is set correctly. We camera the line first and avoid jetting pipe that is broken or collapsed.

    For most modern and well-maintained pipes, hydro jetting is very safe. However, for very old, corroded, or severely damaged pipes, the high pressure could cause further damage. That is why GNV Plumbing always performs a camera inspection first to check your pipe's condition and confirm hydro jetting is appropriate.

    Small consumer jetters exist, but they lack the pressure and flow of professional units. High-pressure water can also injure untrained users and harm fragile pipe.

    Key signs you might need hydro jetting include frequently recurring clogs in the same drain, multiple slow drains throughout your home, persistent foul odors coming from drains, or gurgling sounds from toilets or drains. These often indicate a significant buildup or blockage that needs more than just a snake.

    A standard residential hydro jetting service in Wilmington usually takes between 1 and 3 hours. This includes the initial camera inspection, the jetting process itself, and a final inspection. More extensive commercial jobs or severe main line blockages might require more time.
