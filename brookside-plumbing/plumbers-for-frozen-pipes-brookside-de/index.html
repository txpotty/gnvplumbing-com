    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbers for Frozen Pipes in Brookside

    Thawing alone usually runs a couple hundred dollars. Night and weekend calls cost more. The price depends on how hard the frozen section is to reach, how long it takes to thaw, and whether the line needs repair once it is flowing again, which we quote before starting that part.

    Check which fixtures work. The frozen section sits between the last working fixture and the first dead one. A dead sink on an outside wall usually means ice in that wall, which a thermal camera can confirm.

    For an exposed pipe you can reach, yes, carefully. Open the faucet first. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer or a space heater kept a safe distance away, working from the faucet back toward the frozen section, and never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame near framing.

    A frost-free hose bib has a long stem that shuts off water inside the warm part of the wall. The outside part drains when you close it. It only works if the hose is disconnected, because a connected hose traps water in the stem where it can freeze and split the valve inside the wall, which you may not notice until spring.

    Disconnect hoses, cover outdoor faucets and backflow preventers, and let a faucet drip on very cold nights. Open sink cabinets on outside walls. If you leave town during cold weather, keep the heat at 55 degrees or higher and consider shutting off the main and draining the lines, which is the most reliable way to avoid coming home to a flooded house.

    A new freeze is not a warranty issue, but it is a warning sign. The line is still too cold. We quote a permanent fix, since patching the same run every winter costs more.

    Keep cold air away from the pipe. Insulation and air sealing do most of the work. Close crawlspace vents for the season, insulate attic and garage runs, swap old hose bibs for frost-free models, and add self-regulating heat cable on any run that still gets too cold despite insulation.

    Most thawing jobs take one to two hours. Burst repairs take one to four hours. Repairs inside finished ceilings or walls take longer because we open the drywall, repair the pipe, test it and leave the opening clean and ready for a patch, and multiple splits on one run add time as well.
